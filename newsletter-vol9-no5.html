With five weeks of the fantasy season completed, we now have nine weeks left for teams to make a push for the postseason. There are many storylines to cover and some will have to wait until a different newsletter. For the sake of my time, I have written some quick hitters below on updates that came out of last week’s results.

As per usual, the standings are posted below along with the points leader race chart. In last week’s newsletter, I alluded to a write-up I had planned for this week. Well, it’s time to check the oven to see what’s been cookin’ the past week. What you’ll find is a historical comparison of every NARPS franchise to that of a NFL franchise. Keep reading to see where your team stands.

Quick Hitters

  • The Deplorables put up the highest scoring week in 2026, boasting a score of 156.8. Josh Allen and Jahmyr Gibbs have accounted for 45% of the total points scored for Sizemore’s team.
  • Sizemore and Cummings become the 3rd and 4th team, respectively, to reach 20,000 career points. Sizemore passed Sonny in Week 5 to move into 2nd place in all-time scoring. Luke Rapp and Tyler Clay are now in a race to become the 5th franchise to reach this mark.
  • The Money Badgers fell seven spots this week and currently sits in last place. The Ravers continue to hold onto the 6th place wild card spot, leading the next team by 21.9 points.
  • The ACLiens ended their 8-game losing streak and avoided tying for the longest losing streak (9) in League history. The current longest winning streak (4) belongs to the Powerhouse. After starting 2-0, the Ravers now have the longest current losing streak (3).
  • The average highest score each week is currently 136.6. The Deplorables have been the highest scoring team three times this year while the Piemakers and Powerhouse each have one highest scoring week thus far.

Every NARPS Franchise, as an NFL Franchise

As you know, I’ve been compiling information and feeding it to Claude to build this website. One day when working on some updates, and idea popped into my head. If we had to compare each NARPS franchise to a NFL team, which would be the most apt comparison? Instead of trying to do this myself, I decided to prompt Claude to complete this task.

Below, you will find Claude’s responses to the prompt. It has all the information regarding our teams and could easily pull NFL data to make this comparison. After running through each of its steps, it spit out the below responses. Now of course, you need to double check if the answers are sane and reasonable. So, I went through and added my own commentary about each response. The teams are ordered by number of titles, championship appearances, playoff wins, and then regular season wins. Let’s get started!

4 titles · 6 title games

1. Prino's Powerhouse Joe Prino

NFL comp: New England Patriots

Claude’s take: The league's dynasty. He has 4 titles (2012, 2015, 2017, 2018), a 4-2 record in title games and the best playoff record in the league at 17-9. He also has the highest points per game (101.5) and the best point differential (+4.3). Like the Patriots, his run was concentrated in one stretch, and there's still a weird down year in it: last place in 2016.

Commish’s comment: Claude nailed this first comparison. Not only do the Powerhouse and Patriots have multiple rings, fans around the world root for these team’s demise. And just when you think a new era has arrived and another team will form a dynasty, the Patriots and Powerhouse appear out of the woodworks to remind us why they are considered the most achieved franchises.

3 titles · 5 title games

2. ACLiens Luke Rapp

NFL comp: Pittsburgh Steelers

Claude’s take: An early dynasty with staying power. He has 3 titles (2014, 2016, 2024) and the most regular-season #1 finishes (4), all from 2011 to 2016. He then slid to last place in 2019 and came back to win it all in 2024, the way the Steelers' 70s dynasty was followed by later titles. He also owns the league's best single game (184).

Commish’s comment: As a Steelers, I appreciate that the black and yellow are viewed positively in terms of a winning legacy. This feels like an apt comparison especially when looking at the span between the second and third championships for the ACLiens. The Steelers dominated in the 70s (four Super Bowls) and lost their winning ways over the next decade. Then, in the 2000s the Steelers won another two rings. The ACLiens had some lost years between 2019-2021, never finishing better than 10th. Then in 2024, they returned to glory.

2 titles · 4 title games

3. Money Badgers Tyler Clay

NFL comp: Baltimore Ravens

Claude’s take: He's always in the mix. He has 2 titles almost exactly a decade apart (2013, 2023), 10 playoff trips, a .518 win % and a 2-2 record in title games. The Ravens won their titles in 2000 and 2012, and both franchises are reliable without ever being a dynasty.

Commish’s comment: Has Claude picked up on my bias against the Money Badgers being a dynasty? Clearly, the Badgers always find themselves in the mix and I think this franchise leans more towards being a staple than not. From 2021 to 2023, Clay led his furry Badgers to 2nd, 3rd, and. 1st place finishes, respectively. When I think of the Ravens, I think of a franchise that puts together a solid team, can deal with bumps in the road, and is competitive nearly every year. This is also true for the Badgers.

2 titles · 2 title games

4. Business as Usual Travis West

NFL comp: New York Giants (2007–11)

Claude’s take: He gets in rarely but wins when he does. He has only 4 playoff appearances, a .422 win % and a -4.6 point differential. Despite that, he's 2-0 in title games and 8-2 in the playoffs (2019, 2021). He's the league's Eli Manning underdog run.

Commish’s comment: Perfect comparison. Every time I see this team’s all-time regular season win % and then look at his playoff win %, it makes me chuckle. He has a .422 regular season win % and boasts an .800% playoff win % . He has won championships (2) in half of his playoff appearances (4). It always seems like this team is stumbling along, then suddenly everything clicks and this train cannot be stopped. The Giants have had goofball players such as Eli and now Jameis which pairs well with the goofiness of this team’s owner.

1 title · 5 title games

5. George's Gangstas Sonny Wright

NFL comp: Dallas Cowboys

Claude’s take: He won it all in the league's very first year (2011) and owns the best all-time win % (.573). He keeps winning regular seasons but goes 10-11 in the playoffs and 1-4 in title games, with runner-up finishes in 2012, 2015, 2019 and 2022. The glory is old, and every January ends the same way.

Commish’s comment: I like the shot Claude is taking at the Cowboys, though I think it’s too dismissive of the Gangstas playoff success. Five championship appearances is impressive, and reminds me of the Bills four Super Bowl losses (though the Bills lost in four consecutive appearances). The Gangstas have historically rostered a high-powered offense similar to teams in Cowboys' history, so this comparison is close to the mark. To me, the Gangstas are a legitimate threat every year, unlike the fraudsters we see in Dallas. I think the Rams or Colts would have been a better comparison for George and his Gangstas.

1 title · 3 title games

6. The Deplorables Zach Sizemore

NFL comp: Philadelphia Eagles

Claude’s take: The perennial contender who finally broke through. He has 11 playoff trips (tied for the most) and 3 top-scoring seasons (also tied for the most). He finished runner-up twice and third three times before the 2025 title, and he's leading the league again in 2026.

Commish’s comment: This comparison is justified. Remember those Donavon McNabb led Eagles that made four straight NFC Championship games? The Deplorables are similar in the same way having knocked on the door several times in before finally breaking through. Just like the Eagles, the Deplorables meddle about between post-season runs until they find their groove once more and compete for a few years as the league’s best team.

1 title · 1 title game

7. Free Agents Z Hickman

NFL comp: Tampa Bay Buccaneers

Claude’s take: The league's boom-or-bust franchise. He has 3 regular-season #1 finishes and 3 last-place finishes, including back-to-back lasts in 2021 and 2022 right after his 2020 title. He even won his title in the same season the Bucs won theirs.

Commish’s comment: I love this comparison. The Free Agents are truly a boom or bust team and each year I never know which way they will go. The same can be true with the Buccaneers. Year in and year out I do not know what sort of product the Bucs will display on the turf. Also, in my view, I see the Bucs as a neutral NFL team. I don’t necessarily root for them nor against them, but at times they are fund to watch. Even when Brady led them to a Super Bowl, I think most of us were in awe of such a run. The same is true for the Free Agents.

1 title · 1 title game

8. Arabian Stallions Walid Salameh

NFL comp: New Orleans Saints

Claude’s take: Long stretches of mediocrity, with a .463 win % and last-place finishes in 2018 and 2020. He did get one title (2022), and he's 5-4 in the playoffs, which means he gets to wave the banner forever.

Commish’s comment: Another great comparison. I like this one not only for its postseason similarities, but also the similarities of New Orleans and the Stallions’ owner. Each brings high-energy, and both have qualities of being wild cards. Just like the Saints, the Stallions made the most of their only championship appearance by taking home the trophy in 2022. This team is not afraid to swing for the fences, often times coming up short when doing so. Regardless, even in down years, both these teams put respect on their names.

0 titles · 2 title games

9. Fantasy Football Team Aaron Burns

NFL comp: Carolina Panthers

Claude’s take: A later arrival (13 seasons) with no title and a .463 win %. He's 0-2 in title games (runner-up in 2023 and 2025) and had a regular-season #1 in 2021, matching the Panthers' two Super Bowl losses exactly.

Commish’s comment: Claude has given another great answer here. The Panthers typically do not pose much of a threat year-to-year, much like the Fantasy Football Team who has only 4 playoff appearances. Though, both teams have been able to make it twice to the championship game without taking home the trophy. The expansion team comparison fits well. I think you could also compare Burns to the Panthers' NFC South counterpart, the Atlanta Falcons. The Falcons are also 0-2 in the Super Bowl and do not have much success year-to-year.

0 titles · 1 title game

10. Lincoln Legends Tyler Cummings

NFL comp: Minnesota Vikings

Claude’s take: I'm sorry. He has 9 playoff trips and a regular-season #1 in 2025, with zero titles and a 5-10 playoff record. His only title-game trip ended in a loss (2014). It's good enough to keep fans believing and never quite enough.

Commish’s comment: This may be giving me too much credit as the Vikings have been and lost all four Super Bowl appearances compared to only one appearance for the Legends. The only finals appearance by this team was 12 years ago which feels just as long as the four Vikings Super Bowl appearances in 1970s. The Vikings and Legends do put up strong regular seasons, but clearly neither have any hardware to show for it. For example, the Legends finished 13-1 last year and didn’t make the championship game. In 2024, the Vikings went 14-3 and lost in the first round of the playoffs.

0 titles · 0 title games

11. Kareem Pie Matt Geiger / Seth Thacker

NFL comp: Los Angeles Chargers

Claude’s take: He had real talent and a sky-high ceiling: 168.7 points is the third-highest single game in league history, behind only Rapp's 184 and Sizemore's 170.8. He made the playoffs 7 times in 15 seasons, but January never cooperated: 2-9 in the playoffs, a best finish of 3rd (2015), and last-place finishes in 2013, 2014 and 2024. Through all of it he showed up every season from 2011 to 2025 as one of the league's originals.

Commish’s comment: Another solid comparison here. The Chargers have done little in the post-season which is also true of the Piemakers. Both teams also seem to show promise when entering a season, yet they seem to flounder when they take the field. They still show doses of talent and skill, but they usually do not pose a serious threat to their opponents.

0 titles · 0 title games

12. Route 2 Ravers Joe Garton

NFL comp: Detroit Lions (pre-Dan Campbell)

Claude’s take: He has 2 playoff trips in 15 seasons, the lowest points per game (93.7) and the worst differential (-6.7). He did start 2026 at 2-0, though, so a full turnaround isn't ruled out.

Commish’s comment: I love that Claude distinguished the Lions as the pre-Dan Campbell teams. We all know how much suffering Lions fans went through before the knee-capper arrived, and Ravers across the Route 2 valley have hollered in despair through similar disappointment. The Ravers are just as lovable as the Lions and maybe Garton has found his Dan Campbell era after a hot start in 2026. With no championship appearances and little to no playoff success, both franchises look to the future for better days ahead.
