A Generation Crippled — Week 2 Recap

Volume 9, Issue 2 · September 24, 2026

The historical scoring pace of Week 1 across the NFL came to a halt in Week 2. In Week 1, there were 791 points scored league-wide, including 93 touchdowns — an NFL Week 1 record. That number fell to 647 points and 69 touchdowns in Week 2. Teams across the League of NARPS were not immune to the dip, and the numbers back it up: NARPS teams averaged 106.5 points in Week 1 and just 91.1 points in Week 2.

Part of the scoring dry spell can be attributed to a generation that got crippled out on the playing field this past weekend. In the fourth quarter of the Vikings-Bears game, Caleb Williams went down in agony with a non-contact hamstring injury. Then, in the Sunday afternoon slate, Jayden Daniels' elbow tried to move in ways that are not natural for the human body. Finally, Monday Night Football gave us Jaxson Dart sustaining what looks like a serious knee injury. In one weekend, the 1st and 2nd overall picks of the 2024 NFL Draft and the 25th pick of the 2025 draft are all out for at least some period of time.

Week 2 LoN Recap

0-2

ACLiens — Luke Rapp

Well, this team is living up to its injury-prone name. AJ Brown was placed on IR, and Caleb Williams and JK Dobbins both left their games with hamstring injuries while Caleb Douglas exited with an ankle injury. Six of eight players scored fewer than 8 points, and five of those scored fewer than 5. The two bright spots continue to be JSN and Chris Olave, a deadly fantasy duo who will need to carry the rest of the roster. This team searches for a better supporting cast heading into Week 3 as it tries to snap a 6-game losing streak.

1-1

Arabian Stallions — Walid Salameh

The Stallions had the misfortune of drawing the Deplorables in Week 2 and took the loss despite scoring the second-most points in the league. Dak Prescott and Amon-Ra St. Brown combined for over 60 points, while Omarion Hampton and D'Andre Swift each cleared double digits and will help anchor this team going forward. The core of this roster is one of the better ones in the League of NARPS, and a strong playoff push looks likely.

0-2

Business as Usual — Travis West

Nothing worse than an 0-2 start to the fantasy season. Malik Nabers played through an injury on Monday night before handing off to backup Jameis Winston, and the pair combined for only 0.6 points. Dallas Goedert left the game in the second quarter with a sprained MCL, and Rhamondre Stevenson mustered just 3.1 points now that TreVeyon Henderson has returned to action. The lone bright spot belonged to CeeDee Lamb, who put up a monstrous 31.3 points. Quinshon Judkins should see better days ahead once the Browns' quarterback situation settles down — though that's been a problem in Cleveland for decades. This team will be desperate for a win in Week 3.

2-0

The Deplorables — Zach Sizemore

The reigning champs are off to the best start in the league so far in 2026. Just like Week 1, Josh Allen and Jahmyr Gibbs powered this team to victory, scoring 40.7 and 20.3 points respectively. Isiah Likely popped off in Week 1; this week it was Davante Adams' turn, scoring 35.5 points on Monday night. The Deplorables also secured their 21st career weekly scoring title, moving into a tie for third place all-time. With Michael Penix Jr. coming back, Drake London should see a spike in production too. Watch out, NARPS — the Deplorables are here to wreck the league again in 2026.

1-1

The Fantasy Football Team — Aaron Burns

The Football Team was on its way to a 2-0 start until Jaxson Dart went down on the Giants' first drive of Monday Night Football. With Dart out, the Football Team fell by 3.5 points. James Cook showed out in Thursday's game against the Lions, rushing for 135 yards and a touchdown, but teammate DJ Moore left the game injured with a -0.1 fantasy score. Garrett Wilson, George Kittle, and Stefon Diggs all scored 14+ points in the loss and will need to help carry this team as it sorts through some injuries.

1-1

Free Agents — Z Hickman

The Free Agents secured their first win of 2026. No single player dominated, but consistency across the board was enough to cruise to a win. Patrick Mahomes connected with Travis Kelce for a touchdown, and Derrick Henry continued his strong start with another 16.2 points. This team used a 6th-round pick on Drake Maye but has already benched last year's MVP runner-up in favor of Mahomes — so far, the move has paid off. The Free Agents could have bolstered the roster with Jaylen Warren, Mike Evans, or Christian Watson in that same 6th round, but this team is finding its groove heading into Week 3.

1-1

George's Gangstas — Sonny Wright

Kenneth Walker's monster Week 1 performance had the Gangstas riding high; in Week 2, this team came back down to earth. To make matters worse, Saquon Barkley left the game injured and Trevor Lawrence put up a stinker in Denver. DeVonta Smith showcased his new role as the Eagles' number-one receiver, hauling in 10 catches for 117 yards and a touchdown. With Caleb Williams' injury, Gangstas WR Luther Burden will likely take a hit in production. This team isn't in freefall yet, but a few results over the next few weeks will go a long way toward buying in.

1-1

Kareem Pie — Seth Thacker

After scoring the most points in Week 1, this team was the league's lowest scorer in Week 2. Call it a fluke — Bijan Robinson managed only 9.6 points and David Montgomery was limited to 3.4. Jeremiyah Love will look to get going in Week 3 after a disappointing showing. The Piemakers benched Jaylen Waddle following a rough season opener, but the move backfired when Waddle put up 17.8 points. Expect the starting lineup to keep getting refined as the season goes on — this team should be more competitive than Week 2 suggested.

1-1

Lincoln Legends — Tyler Cummings

A week after the Legends' 10-game win streak came to an end, they bounced back to secure their 100th career win. Ja'Marr Chase used some miraculous footwork to score his first touchdown of the season, then added a second later in the game. Dalton Kincaid is currently the TE1 in fantasy and scored 19.0 points on Thursday night, while Chuba Hubbard is settling in nicely as the lead back for the Panthers and the Legends, finding the end zone three times already this season. Continued wins will require more consistency from Rashee Rice and George Pickens, plus better outputs from Lamar Jackson.

1-1

Money Badgers — Tyler Clay

Fresh off the waiver wire, Tyler Shough got the start for the Money Badgers in Week 2 and delivered a solid 22.3 points. Christian McCaffrey didn't disappoint, putting up a 20-burger, and the two-tight-end setup is working out so far with both Tyler Warren and Trey McBride finding the end zone. Speaking of the Cardinals — did anyone see Marvin Harrison Jr.? He needs a complete career reset; his 0 points in Week 2 mark his fourth straight regular-season game without multiple receptions. Meanwhile, Ashton Jeanty was a bit hobbled against the Chargers and Alec Pierce left the game against the Chiefs. This team is stumbling but got a much-needed win after its opponent's quarterback left the game injured.

1-1

Prino's Powerhouse — Joe Prino

The Powerhouse benched Justin Herbert in favor of Brock Purdy, and the move paid off — Purdy scored 28.4 points to Herbert's 7.8. Prino's Powerhouse was fortunate to draw the Piemakers this week after failing to crack 80 points for the second straight week. Brock Bowers is hopeful to play in Week 3, and TreVeyon Henderson returned in Week 2 to put up 13.6 points, but the injury bug continues to plague this team after Puka Nacua missed the week and may miss the next one too. The Powerhouse needs just 64.7 more points to become the first franchise in league history to reach 20,000 career regular-season points.

2-0

Route 2 Ravers — Joe Garton

The Ravers' QB, Jayden Daniels, went down with an injury that made even Tom Brady curse out loud. Despite losing him before halftime, the Ravers put in a team effort to take down the Gangstas in Week 2. Jonathan Taylor continued building on his record-setting 2025 season with a 27.2-point performance against the Chiefs. The Ravers will need Terry McLaurin and Matthew Golden to start producing soon if this team wants to make a playoff run. For now, the Ravers move to 2-0 and look to keep the momentum rolling into Week 3.
