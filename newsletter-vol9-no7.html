The Mid-Season Review

Volume 9, Issue 7 · October 31, 2026

Welcome to the halfway point of the 2026 fantasy season. This Sunday, we will turn the page to November and frostier weather will soon encompass the landscape (at least for most of our readers). As per usual, this week’s newsletter includes the current standings and the points leader race chart. We’ve had an exciting first half and some teams look to solidify their position. Other teams will hope the second half bodes better than the first.

As stated last week, I have completed a Mid-Season Review for every team. But before we get further on this subject, I want to call out this season’s NFL Survivor results. Heading into Week 7, Zach Sizemore and Aaron Burns were the only participants remaining, each with one loss. Burns selected the New York Jets as they took on their divisional opponent, the Miami Dolphins. Well, surprisingly, the Dolphins got their first win this week and knocked Burns out of the pool. Meanwhile, Sizemore chose the Houston Texans as they were 10.5-point favorites over the New York Giants. As we now know, JJ McCarthy led the Giants to an overtime win against the Texans, eliminating Sizemore.

Since both teams received their second loss during the same week, they have been crowned 2026 NFL Survivor Co-Champs. With a pool of $600, they each take home $300. Good job to Zach and Aaron for making it just long enough to beat out your fellow NARPS.

The Powerhouse has won five out of their last six matchups after posting the League’s lowest score this season (66.9) in Week 1. The Powerhouse’s 11th round draft pick, Brock Purdy, has certainly been the MVP of this team averaging 20.7 points. Another great value pick by Prino occurred in the 6th round, drafting Steelers RB Jaylen Warren. Warren is RB11 for the year and will look to continue his great season even as Rico Dowdle returns from injury. Then, the first two picks by this team, Puka Nacua and Kyren Williams, bring the voltage that stuns their opponents. Let’s not forget that Tee Higgins has scored more than his Bengals teammate, Ja’Marr Chase, giving the Powerhouse another weapon. Finally, just when you think this team can’t get better, Brock Bowers shows up to deal the knockout blow.

I don’t like writing this next part, but I think the Powerhouse has the strongest team as we head into the second half of the season. This team demonstrated its potential in Weeks 3-5, scoring over 120 points in each of those weeks. Now that their stars are healthy, the championship feels like it’s theirs for the taking. We all know the postseason experience Prino brings, and so NARPS across the league will have to put up their best efforts if they want to stop the Powerhouse.

The Deplorables are coming off their worst week yet this season, putting up 69.8 points. This is their second week of scoring less than 100. The Deplorables have the playmakers that are needed to help this team win back-to-back championships. Josh Allen is averaging 26.7 points/game and Jahmyr Gibbs is close behind at 25.8. Sizemore and his deplorable bunch actually are the opposite of their namesake. This team boasts solid depth, though the injury to Davante Adams will certainly lower this team’s ceiling. Drake London should be able to help lift this team, having averaged 15.5 points since Week 3, and has only scored one touchdown during the season. Isaiah Likely is TE2 on the year and has been a shining star for this team. Finally, if Deebo Samuel can return after his Week 8 bye, he could add much needed firepower to keep the Deplorables comfortably in front.

For me, this team is on par with the Powerhouse as far as chances to win it all this year. However, the injury to Davante Adams does take some wind out of their sails. The depth of this roster will help ensure to smooth out any bumps in the road. Especially if Rico Dowdle and Kyle Monangai can produce as the number two RBs for their teams. This team will still rely on Allen and Gibbs to post big games, and it’s those kind of players you need to win it all.

The Free Agents are second in scoring, and tied for first in median record thanks to the likes of a slew of players. The linchpin for this team is Derrick Henry, who is RB8 for the year averaging 16.8 points. However, we cannot overlook the collective effort from all the players on this roster. After ditching Drake Maye in Week 2 in favor of Mahomes, the Free Agents went back to Maye in Week 5 where he posted 39.3 points that week. Meanwhile, Travis Kelce keeps chugging along and reminds us that he is Mahomes’ comfort blanket. Zay Flowers is scoring 17.0 points/game in games he’s played. Rounding out this bunch is Tony Pollard (35 points scored last week), Tet McMillan (38.2 points in Week 4), and Ollie Gordon (averaging 15.4 points since Week 3). Luckily for the Free Agents, they were able to snag Ollie Gordon from the waiver wire after their 1st round pick, De’Von Achane, went down with a season-ending injury.

I must admit, I was not sold on this team after the draft, but Hickman has proved this hater wrong. This team has a decent bench to turn to as many of their players approach their bye week. I would not be shocked if this team was bounced in the first round of the playoffs or if they won the whole thing. Thus far, the Free Agents have been playing team ball and have not relied on one single player to get it done. If this team can continue to show that they have strength in numbers, then they could be a serious threat to win it all.

The Gangstas have bounced around in standings all year moving at least 3 spots up or down each week. Right now, they are tied for seventh in median record and tied for sixth for all-play record. Kenneth Walker has been the clear-cut MVP of this team averaging 23.1 points. Meanwhile, Saquon Barkley has been dealing with injuries and has only cracked double digit points once this year. The Gangstas have the Jaguars combo stack of Trevor Lawrence and Parker Washington, and barring one or two weeks, these two have been a solid pairing. Another disappointment for this team is their 4th round pick, Ladd McConkey, who has also only reached double digits once this season. Luther Burden may finally be turning the page on his lackluster season now that he has his starting QB back. Burden has scored 35.2 points over the past two weeks. Tucker Kraft has not been something to write home about, yet he is TE7 for 2026. Meanwhile, Blake Corum is the Gangstas RB2 and has scored at least 13 points in his last three matchups.

The Gangstas are in a good position to make the postseason, but health issues linger over this team especially regarding Saquon. Other teams can reach to their bench to help cover injuries, but George does not have many viable options to turn to. If Jonathan Taylor does end up getting traded, then Seth McGowan could help steer this team in the right direction. If this team reaches its potential, then it certainly would be a championship contender, but we have yet to see the full potential due to injuries and inconsistent play from these wannabe Gangstas.

The Route 2 Ravers have had unfortunate scheduling luck this year. Fortunately, our 6th place qualifying rule means this team still has hope. Just to demonstrate how the schedule has hurt the Ravers, this team would be 6-1 if they had the Money Badgers schedule (the Badgers have the lowest points against). Instead, this team sits at 2-5 and will more than likely have to qualify via the wild card. That being said, I like this team a lot. Jonathan Taylor, Javonte Williams and Bucky Irving have been leading the charge and are averaging 18.4, 17.3 and 14.4 points, respectively. Matthew Golden is finally showcasing why the Packers selected him in the first round last year, scoring 17+ points in four matchups this year. The team’s weakest spot is the WR2 and TE position. Mark Andrews has been less than sub par and this week the Ravers are starting Malik Washington at WR. The Ravers have had good QB play between CJ Stroud and Jared Goff, though it looks like they are rolling out former Mountaineer, Geno Smith, this week.

I like this team’s chances if they can make the playoffs. If Breece Hall can return to full health, then the Route 2 Ravers will have a lot of firepower. However, they will have to fend off a lot of teams that are competing for the 6th playoff spot. But I would bet on this team over most of the others. The Ravers are the only team this year whose lowest scoring week has not gone below 80, and it’s this consistency that could propel them to the playoffs and maybe to their first ever championship.

The Piemakers got off to a 3-1 start but have now lost two matchups over the past three weeks. Much like the Gangstas, the Piemakers have also been up and down both in the win column and points scored. Since acquiring Bryce Young from the waiver wire in Week 3, he has been in competition for the starting QB role with Joe Burrow. For the most part, the newcomer to the League has started the right one. Bijan has anchored this team with three weeks of 27+ points. Drafted in the 6th round, Christian Watson is having the best season of his career and is only 36 receiving yards away from his highest single-season mark and he has already matched his TD total (6) from last year. The rest of this team has not been reliable with Jeremiyah Love, Nico Collins, and David Montgomery dealing with injuries. Jaylen Waddle has yet to break through with his new team, and Kyle Pitts is doing what he does best (confusing fantasy owners all over the world) by scoring a total of 9.2 points over the first four weeks and 43.9 points in the last three weeks.

This team is scrappy just like its owner on the basketball court, so I can’t count them out. However, I don’t see enough firepower for this team to win it all. If they do want to make a run, then Nico Collins will need to play like a WR1, Love needs to get healthy, Pitts needs to have consistency, and Waddle needs to show up. The Piemakers have stashed Jordyn Tyson on the IR all season and his return could maybe give this team the juice it needs to make a playoff push.

This team is in a strange pattern. The first week they scored 111.5 points then failed to reach 72 points in the following two weeks. Then in Week 4, they exploded for 130.4. The following two weeks they entered another slump not breaking 79 points. Last week, they had another good performance scoring 116.7. Starting in a 2-5 hole means this team will need to break this pattern and put up more consistent scoring in hopes of securing a wild card spot. The return of Josh Jacobs is much needed for this team as Chase Brown has failed to live up to his 2nd round selection. Despite one bad week, CeeDee Lamb continues to prove why he is the WR1 in Dallas, averaging 18.4. Quinshon Judkins and Rhamondre Stevenson will need more weeks like Week 7 if this team is to secure a postseason spot as both players got off to a slow start at the beginning of the season. Since Week 4, Malik Nabers is averaging over 20 points/game and has inserted some energy into Business as Usual. It’s strange seeing Kirk Cousins as a starting fantasy QB in 2026, but with Jalen Hurts out for the season, Cousins has been more than serviceable and is QB6 for the year.

I like this team. If they can make the postseason, I would not want to face them. To me, I think Business as Usual is heading in the right direction after a tumultuous start. If Chase Brown can return some production, then the other cast of characters will certainly welcome the relief. I expect this team to dig out of the hole it created and to make a serious push for the 6th and final playoff spot.

The Fantasy Football Team (FFT) has had a solid season thus far. Their highest scoring total came in Week 1 (121.2) and their lowest scoring mark just occurred this past week (78.9). In between those weeks, this team put up some consistent performances ranging from 83 to 117 points and are tied for 3rd for both median record and all-play record. FFT is led by James Cook who is RB10 and averages 18.1 points. Unfortunately, their second round pick, Justin Jefferson, has only scored 60.6 points this season and has yet to reach 100 receiving yards in a game. Travis Etienne’s injury has made a hole in the boat that has been difficult to plug. However, FFT has had a few plug and play options that have kept their vessel afloat with the likes of Kyler Murray, Emanuel Wilson, and Germie Bernard coming to the rescue. George Kittle is scoring 12.5 points/game which makes him TE3 and a solid 6th round pick by Burns.

I’m not sure how much juice there is to squeeze out of this team and I’m afraid better days are now behind them. But what do I know? Maybe rostering three tight ends is the savvy fantasy move (doubtful). Maybe Garrett Wilson shows more consistency week-to-week (possible). Maybe DJ Moore finally returns to full health (unlikely). By the time we get to the end of this season, I would bet there’s a better chance last year’s runner up won’t be in the playoffs.

If the Legends do make the playoffs, I expect an early exit which is par for the course for this franchise.
